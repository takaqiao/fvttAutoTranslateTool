[16] Heading 1 | ❗ Warning – PF2e Rituals
[17] normal | 
[18] normal | There’s a big issue with Ritual rules, which I need to warn you about immediately: They’re way too hard to cast, and the math skews results heavily toward failure.
[19] normal | Issue 1: Rituals are almost always designed on-level, but require a Very Hard DC primary check, for example DC 24 to Open the Wall of Ghosts at level 4-5.
[20] normal | Issue 2: Despite the primary caster already needing to roll really well, secondary casters provide +0 on a Success, -4 on a Failure, and an effective -14 (!!) on a Critical Failure.
[21] normal | Flavour-wise, it feels like a “secondary caster” should make a ritual easier. Mechanically, it is the opposite! If a single caster rolls poorly, the whole ritual will almost certainly fail.
[22] normal | Example: A sample level 4 party with +12 Primary, and +11/10/9 Secondary casters trying to Open the Wall of Ghosts only succeeds less than 30% of the time (and crit fails about 25% of the time).
[23] normal | tl;dr These ritual rules are awful. Paizo knows it, because the hardcover version has added a bandaid solution (Page 310): Reducing the DCs to cast a ritual by 2 to 5 with each repeated attempt.
[24] normal | This still makes the process an unsatisfying crapshoot of trial-and-error, and will average several fails and crit fails before a success. In the 5th-rank Transmigrate ritual the PCs will cast at the end of Book 3, it still means the entire fate of Willowshore could come down to just one bad d20 roll!!
[25] normal | I highly recommend the following houserules to rituals:
[26] normal | Secondary Casters provide a +2 / +1 / -2 / -4 untyped bonus on a Crit / Success / Fail / Crit Fail.
[27] normal | Hero Points can be used on ritual checks.

[29] Heading 1 | ❗ Complex Hazards
[30] normal | 
[31] normal | Complex hazards are theoretically one of the coolest things in the PF2 system – unfortunately, they’re often poorly used in adventures.
[32] normal | Complex hazards have the potential to be very exciting fight mechanics, which force your PCs to use tactics other than “beat up enemies until every health bar hits 0”. But, in practice, almost every complex hazard can be disabled with 1 skill check, which makes them extremely anticlimactic.
[33] normal | Especially in SoG, because Religion works on every single haunt in this book, and maxing out your Religion is kind of a no-brainer in this AP.
[34] normal | Here is a mechanic I use in my games, which you can steal to make SoG more exciting:
[35] normal | 
[36] normal | Suppress Haunt (1a to 3a) (concentrate)
[37] normal | Frequency once per round
[38] normal | Effect You interact with an ongoing complex haunt to suppress it. Attempt a Religion check, or another appropriate check (GM discretion). At the GM's discretion, this may require you to be in a particular location, touch a particular object, or may gain other traits like manipulate or mental. The DC is determined by the number of actions:
[39] normal | (1a) Very Hard DC for the haunt’s level
[40] normal | (2a) Standard DC for the haunt’s level
[41] normal | (3a) Easy DC for the haunt’s level
[42] normal | The GM may adjust the DC further for particularly effective or ineffective tactics.
[43] normal | Critical Success The party achieves 2 Suppression Points (SP).
[44] normal | Success The party achieves 1 SP.
[45] normal | Failure You fail to suppress the haunt.
[46] normal | Critical Failure The party loses 1 SP. If the party already had 0 SP, you instead take a -2 circumstance penalty to your AC or saving throw against the haunt’s next ability.
[47] normal | 
[48] normal | Since stealing this from Doomsday Dawn inventing this a few years ago, I’ve truly used it in every single campaign I run. The difficulty can be adjusted as follows:
[49] normal | For an ordinary haunt (for example, E3: Main Hall), disable it at 2 or 3 SP.
[50] normal | For a challenging haunt, disable it at 4 or 5 SP.
[51] normal | For the most difficult and unique haunts (for example, on boss fights, or the haunt Within the Wall of Ghosts), the haunt cannot be permanently disabled. Instead, the haunt is weakened for 1 round when it reaches 1 SP (e.g. -2 or -4 to its attacks or DCs), or disabled for 1 round when it reaches 2 SP. It resets its SP to 0 at the start of its turn.
[52] normal | These most powerful haunts can only be deactivated permanently by achieving some other objective in the encounter, but Suppress Haunt will still help the PCs defend against it.
[53] normal | It is important to avoid confusion or a feeling that the encounter is “unfair” or “there’s nothing we can do”, so explain the mechanic clearly and openly!!

[60] Heading 2 | Pacing – Avoid Too Much Filler!
[61] normal | 
[62] normal | Unlike Book 1, Book 2 opts for a series of “Weekly Events” to give you enough sidequests to fill all 3 months of fall.
[63] normal | Some players will love every weekly event, some will start to feel like it’s filler. YMMV.
[64] normal | Personally, I think these events work really well at the start of the book, slowly building tension, and providing small distractions as the players research the Open the Wall of Ghosts ritual and earn necessary Preparation Points.
[65] normal | However, sometime in Week 4, 5, 6, maybe 7, your PCs will inevitably finish their research and leave for the Monastery, where they will find THE BIG REVEAL that is the crux of this AP.
[66] normal | After coming back from this, with new, earth-shattering information and several leads swimming in their head, the book seems to expect you to….. go back to downtime and keep running Weekly Events until Fall is done. 🤔
[67] normal | IMO this makes for terrible narrative pacing, and I’d recommend considering a time skip (see below).
[68] normal | 
[69] Heading 2 | Cutting Events
[70] normal | 
[71] normal | Before starting Fall, it may be a good idea to look ahead at all the weekly events and only pick your 6-8 favourites ahead of time. Instead of forcing your players to grind through all 12 of them, just skip through some weeks here and there.
[72] normal | If you do, you can rearrange and run them in any order.
[73] normal | Remember: the Pilgrim's Path and Monastery are the meat of the book and essentially the climax of the AP. They contain the best encounters, and hugely thematic plot and reveals. Chapter 2 and 3 will be the longest single “arc” your players experience in the whole AP!
[74] normal | I chose to run Haunted Hair, Missing Corpse, and First Long Night before my PCs opened the Wall of Ghosts. Upon returning from the Monastery, the party learned about the Stable Fire and Vanishings that had happened in their absence (getting some opportunity for fun investigation), and then experienced the Faceless Ghost event in one of the last weeks of fall.
[75] normal | Vanished NPCs can potentially return as trapped souls in Book 4, so choose wisely!
[76] normal | 
[77] Heading 2 | Optional: Time Skip
[78] normal | 
[79] normal | When the PCs travel up the Pilgrim’s Path, they are in a parallel mindscape. You can use this opportunity to introduce time dilation – e.g. while they are only gone 1 week, 1 to 2 months pass in Willowshore.
[80] normal | In my case, I did a literal time skip – when they emerged from fighting Xin Yue, time had “slipped” and it was suddenly late fall, with snow falling around them.
[81] normal | This allowed my party to leave Willowshore in Week 4 of Fall and return in Week 11! Thus I could transition from the Monastery into Book 3 without too much delay.

[94] Heading 2 | Foreshadowing
[95] normal | 
[96] normal | The Pilgrim’s Path is the moment to really start ramping up the themes of the AP with hints and foreshadowing
[97] normal | The Bridge, Garden, and Mountain shrines represent the pilgrims’ past, future, and present lives, respectively. (Page 117) (Legacy: Page 31)
[98] normal | There are significantly more temporal anomalies and other magical events here than in Book 1 to hint at the big plot twists.
[99] normal | Some encounters, like the one inside the Wall of Ghosts, are almost entirely metaphorical!

[104] normal | 
[105] normal | Verdict: Dangerous but awesome, 10/10 flavour.
[106] normal | The haunt has a fairly high Reflex DC of 22 and hits the whole map. Any characters that hit Dying (especially if they are Wounded from the Vulture fight) can very quickly die on subsequent rounds (with a -4 to Reflex for being Unconscious).

[111] normal | I suggest erring on the side of overexplaining the mechanics here – if your players don’t know what’s happening (especially if they’re new and don’t recognize a complex hazard), they could easily spend a couple rounds fumbling and get into a death spiral.
[112] normal | This encounter is super cool and thematic – one of the most memorable in the AP. Just be careful to avoid a TPK with what may feel like a “confusing” or “unfair” mechanic.

[160] Heading 2 | Mountain Shrine
[161] normal | 
[162] normal | 
[163] normal | 
[164] normal | Verdict: Highly flavourful and unique encounter that can get very frustrating or borderline unfair depending on party comp and tactics.
[165] normal | Iogaka has nonmagical flight, a 30-ft ranged attack, and can fly even in strong winds.
[166] normal | At level 5, there is virtually no counterplay to that for many parties, and melee players will simply have to resort to ranged attacks (which, on top of being inherently worse for them, also get a -2 from the storm).
[167] normal | Swimming requires a DC 25 (!!) Athletics, which isn’t easy even for high-Str Expert-proficiency swimmers. Untrained PCs will literally drown attempting this.
[168] normal | If she is played 100% optimally, Wall of Wind can even disable the players’ ranged attacks, as she just floats behind it, blasting from complete safety.
[169] normal | Caveat: There are two major hints that were planted earlier for this fight:
[170] normal | 1) Iogaka is Sickened by Incense (pg 119) (Legacy: pg 33)
[171] normal | Unfortunately, in practice, the incense is almost impossible to use given her Fly speed and the high winds, which seems almost like the encounter designer trolling you.
[172] normal | 2) (Legacy) The Anchor Feather Token can stop the raft from moving (pg 30)
[173] normal | I give it 70% odds your party completely forgets they have this token.
[174] normal | This was also removed in the hardcover, giving the party no option to use it at all! Whoops! 🙃
[175] normal | I love the setup of challenging players with stormy weather, and stranding them in the middle of a raft on the lake, needing to find a creative solution to the encounter – but it’s easy for it to become a “no-win” situation where Athletics is useless, the rope is severed, and their ranged options all suck. This can turn unfun (or even TPK) very very quickly.
[176] normal | 
[177] Heading 3 | Recommendations
[178] normal | 
[179] normal | Lower the Swim DC to 15 or 20 (and/or use more creative mechanics, like allowing players to grab the long dangling rope after the pulley breaks). Otherwise, the encounter will likely play out in a fairly static manner as your players simply won’t be able to move.
[180] normal | Then, if your party doesn’t have a lot of ranged damage, either:
[181] normal | A) encourage them to “lure her in” by seeking shelter in the cave, on the stairs, or amidst trees
[182] normal | If you do, make sure the Swim DC is actually possible for them.
[183] normal | B) Plant some sort of consumable(s) earlier on the Path to let them deal with the Flying problem – scrolls of Earthbind or Fly, Emerald Grasshoppers, etc
[184] normal | Making Iogaka more “fair” in this way also allows you to make her stronger! Give her more spells, starting with 3x Rank 2 Thunderstrike.
[185] normal | Extra Challenge – I added even more spells:
[186] normal | 1x Erratic Wisp (Floating Flame reflavoured to air / bludgeoning)
[187] normal | 1x Crackling Lightning Bolt (Like the wand, a 10-ft wide Lightning Bolt that applies Off-guard. She opens combat with it for a flashy and powerful Round 1)
[188] normal | 1x Pressure Zone (This is a 5th-rank spell, but it deals no damage. If you reduce range to 60ft and area to 15ft burst, it’s completely fine at this level)

[195] Heading 1 | Tan Sugi Monastery – General
[196] normal | 
[197] normal | 
[198] normal | 
[199] normal | Overall, the Monastery fights are not as difficult (for their level) as the Pilgrim’s Path.
[200] normal | Especially because there is no time pressure to do 3 encounters in 1 day, and the PCs can retreat and rest at almost any point.
[201] normal | The Monastery is also the first real open-ended “dungeon” the players have experienced! After the scripted Weekly Events, and the completely linear nature of the Pilgrim’s Path, play up that freedom!! Let the PCs go in any direction they wish, try weird stuff, or even “sequence break” the dungeon.
[202] normal | The Monastery environment physically changes throughout the four purifications (Page 131) (Legacy: Page 44) – emphasize how creepy and dilapidated it is initially, to create a noticeable contrast.
[203] normal | 
[204] Heading 2 | Zhi Hui’s Answers
[205] normal | 
[206] normal | Purifying shrines lets the party summon Master Zhi Hui to answer a limited amount of questions before she disappears. This is a fantastic setup! It trickles information and gives a story reason to clear most of the encounters.

Simply asking “what is the Willowshore Curse” makes her immediately reveal the Mindscape, which I find to be extremely anticlimactic. 😐 All my GM instincts say that the big reveal should be saved for before / during the big boss encounter.
[208] normal | As such, I had Zhi Hui stick to answers about Kugaptee and the Monastery, and only revealed the big twist in a final scene just before Xin Yue (see below).

[226] Heading 3 | Optional Narrative Sidebar: Combining the Two Reveals
[227] normal | 
[228] normal | This is a complex and somewhat controversial decision, but I chose to move the “You are actually dead” reveal here, slightly earlier than where it normally happens in Book 3.
[229] normal | My reasoning for this was that I simply did not think my players would believe the explanation “you are alive and have simply been stuck in a time loop” – the first two books have way too many hints about death, ghosts, reincarnation, etc.
[230] normal | I also think that, compared to the climactic buildup and payoff of the journey to the Monastery (and all the fighting required to reach Kugaptee’s Grave), the 2nd reveal – merely taking place in a conversation with the Governor – simply lacks the same punch.
[231] normal | So even if my players did not guess the 2nd reveal immediately, it could still turn out to be a fairly underwhelming follow-up to the 1st one.
[232] normal | I also couldn’t wait to get to the Shinzo conversation in Book 3, and this let me move it several sessions earlier. 🙂
[233] normal | If you don’t want to change this, there’s nothing wrong with running the reveals as written. This is a highly individual decision I leave to each GM.
[234] Heading 1 | Tan Sugi Monastery – Overall Creature Balance
[235] normal | 
[236] normal | There are four types of nindoru in the Monastery: Meokdans (CL 2), Sojiruh (CL 4), Guhdggi (CL 5), and Ijda (CL 6).
[237] normal | Since the PCs should be Level 6, this creates a weird dynamic where the dangerous, scary fiends that have been foreshadowed for 1.5 books are actually… almost all lower level than the PCs, and may feel somewhat underwhelming.
[238] normal | Furthermore, the Monks of Kugaptee are also Level 6 Creatures, but they are incredibly overtuned (see below) and will easily overshadow the nindoru – which feels like another mechanical flavour fail.
[239] normal | Recommendation: Rescale the creatures like this. Use Weak and Elite adjustments, or see below for more advanced breakdowns.
[240] normal | Meokdan 2 (unchanged)
[241] normal | Sojiruh 4 (unchanged)
[242] normal | Kurobozu 5 (lowered by 1)
[243] normal | Guhdggi 6 (increased by 1)
[244] normal | Ijda 7 (increased by 1)
[245] normal | Foundry JSONs
[246] normal | IMO this fits the intended flavour more (the monks should not be scarier than the fiends!), and creates several “miniboss” encounters with the Guhdggi and Ijdas.
[247] normal | 
[248] Heading 3 | 
[249] Heading 2 | Holy vs Unholy
[250] normal | 
[251] normal | Nindoru are in weird design middle ground, where they have the unholy trait like other fiends, but don’t have a holy weakness.
[252] normal | This sort of makes sense flavour-wise, since nindoru are Material Plane fiends and overall do feel “less unholy” than demons or devils.
[253] normal | The problem is, most holy effects don’t actually gain any additional damage against unholy creatures. Holy strikes from a champion, angel eidolon, or Infuse Vitality buff don’t do anything special against nindoru. Holy-sanctified spells like Spiritual Armament or Divine Wrath, likewise, affect a nindoru or a human exactly the same. Overall, this can definitely be very confusing and disappointing to some players.
[254] normal | Only a few effects in the game, like Holy Light, can take advantage of the unholy trait without a weakness.
[255] normal | Optional: I changed all the nindoru by adding a Holy weakness (equal to their Silver weakness), which made fighting them simpler and cooler. YMMV.
[256] normal | Increase their HP by at least 2-4x the Weakness value to compensate.
[257] normal | 
[258] Heading 2 | ❗ Monks of Kugaptee (variant Kurobozu)
[259] normal | 
[260] normal | Oh boy. 😬
[261] normal | The kurobozu is already an absurd stat block. It has high Athletics, high AC, high atk, it’s fast, it has Flurry of Blows + Stunning Fist, and a total of FIVE (!) abilities on its stat block that force a saving throw.
[262] normal | An on-hit DC 24 Fort that causes Stupefied absolutely destroys spellcasters.
[263] normal | Stunning Flurry and Brawling Critical can lead to death spirals where you simply cannot do anything against these creatures if they roll well.
[264] normal | Overall, they put out lots of damage and debuffs, and aren’t easy to kill either.
[265] normal | And then… Paizo decided to buff their stat block in the Hardcover version.
[266] normal | The new “Monks of Kugaptee” have +2 to their Brawling Critical DC, and their fist damage has gone from 1d6+8+1d4 to 2d6+8+1d6… an increase of more than 30%!
[267] normal | I have no words for this except that it is a colossal balance mistake.
[268] normal | Even 1 of these (E14) can be tough. Fighting 2 (E4) is brutal. Fighting 3 (E6) is suicide.
[269] normal | Recommendation: Use the legacy kurobozu, remove Brawling Critical, AND add the Weak adjustment. Then, try to avoid most or all of the fights with RP. (see E4 below)
[270] normal | 
[271] Heading 2 | Guhdggi
[272] normal | 
[273] normal | I love this creature. The art of the guhdggi got more excited and visceral reactions from my players than almost anything else in the whole AP.
[274] normal | Sadly, maybe it’s just bad RNG, but the guhdggi has failed to do anything for me both times I’ve used it. Unlike the kurobozu, which has a million passive and “on-hit” effects, perhaps the guhdggi is too “fair”.
[275] normal | Recommendations: Start with Elite adjustment.
[276] normal | 15ft speed is just too low. If the Giant Toad has 25ft, this can too.
[277] normal | The breath weapon is also too small and should be a 30ft cone.
[278] normal | Remaster Grab is actually a nerf to Swallow Whole creatures. I recommend using the legacy Grab rules to make Cage Prey a bit more likely to land.
[279] normal | Lose the Path at-will (mostly for flavour… it’s the Wandering Nindoru!)
[280] normal | 
[281] Heading 2 | Ijda
[282] normal | 
[283] normal | These large nindoru are SO cool and I really wanted them to feel like Elden Ring bosses
[284] normal | The Slow aura and AoE confusion ability on this are already fairly strong, but I still buffed them further to CL7. Present this fight as a “miniboss”; don’t just spring it on your party.
[285] normal | +1 to all stats and DCs except attack.
